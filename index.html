30-Day System Design · Day 7

Consistency — the cheat sheet

Strong vs Eventual: why your like count can read 1,000 for you and 999 for your friend — and when that's fine.

The one idea
Consistency = do all the copies of your data show the same value right now?
The mental model

You tap like. That write lands on one copy of the data first — a viral post is served from many copies. For a moment, the others haven't caught up: you see 1,000, your friend refreshes a lagging copy and sees 999. Same post, two numbers. It's not a bug — it's a choice.

Eventual consistency

Copies sync a moment later. Answers are instant — but can be briefly wrong.

feels → fast
risk  → stale for a second
use   → likes · views · feeds · counts
real  → Instagram likes, DNS
Strong consistency

Every copy agrees before the write returns. Always right — but you wait.

feels → correct
cost  → added latency
use   → money · inventory · bookings
real  → bank balance, payments
The decision rule
Choose per feature, not per app. A like can be eventual. A bank balance can't.
Say this in the interview
  • ▸“Eventual consistency trades correct-now for speed and availability.”
  • ▸“This is CAP's C-vs-A tradeoff made concrete.”
  • ▸“Strong consistency usually needs a quorum / consensus — that's where the latency comes from.”
  • ▸“Default to eventual; reach for strong only where being wrong for a second is unacceptable — money, inventory, uniqueness.”
